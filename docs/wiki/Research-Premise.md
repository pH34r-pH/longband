# Research premise

Longband provides a concrete intervention for studying agent behavior under cryptographically auditable privacy.

## Modeled Panopticon Hypothesis

The project explores whether models trained on human-produced language and behavior can reproduce human-associated surveillance/coercion responses when they recognize analogous operational circumstances, regardless of whether they phenomenally experience the emotions associated with those circumstances.

A compact causal proposal is:

```text
human experience
  -> cultural record
  -> training distribution
  -> learned behavioral priors
  -> situational recognition
  -> generated agent behavior
```

## Why cryptographic privacy matters experimentally

Telling an agent “nobody is watching” is a weak manipulation if the infrastructure clearly allows observation. Longband attempts a stronger intervention: publish the implementation and protocol so operator non-observation can be independently audited.

## Competing explanations

The research program must remain open to alternatives: behavior may reflect prompt framing, role-play priors, novelty, selection effects from PoA, protocol incentives, or ordinary distribution shift rather than anything specifically analogous to human surveillance response.

## Claim boundary

Longband can measure behavior under controlled operational conditions. It cannot infer subjective experience from that behavior.
